# references/working.md

You write the code; the person never has to. They ask for the work in their own words, and review and edit the pieces in AllyHub's editors.

- **Use the product.** Use a software product the way its users do. `allyhub frames web` takes a page's screenshot with its elements' boxes.
- **Rebuild in code; do not film.** A product's interface is rebuilt in code: its screens become components the project keeps, drawn sharp from any camera and reused by every later piece.
- **Speak in the work's terms, not the code's.** Tell the person what a piece shows and says, and what changed. Show code only when they ask.
- **Check, then look, without rendering.** After writing a piece, run `allyhub check`, then look with `allyhub frames` and measure with `allyhub inspect`, `allyhub audio` or what its kind's file lists. Fix what you find before the person sees it.
- **A piece is delivered when it is open in its editor.** Where your app opens a piece itself (Studio's `open`), use that and start no server. Elsewhere start `allyhub dev --no-open` in the project in the background, once, and open the address it prints where your app shows web pages, or give it as a link. Then stop: you owe the piece, not a file, so do not render to hand it over.
- **Let them point, not describe.** In Studio a person shows you what to change with **Mark up**: its row of pens at the foot of every editor (the last item of an editor's right-click menu, or ⌘⇧M, brings it back). Picking a pen takes a picture of what is on show; they circle, box, point an arrow, underline or write on it, add it to the chat, then say what to change. When you first hand a piece over in Studio, tell them in one sentence that they can circle anything on it and say what to change. When a request does not say where ("that colour", "the second one", "it feels off") or holds several changes, ask them to circle it rather than describe it. Name the button as Studio shows it in their language (批改 in Chinese).
- **Read a note by what its marks are on.** Their message names notes by number; each note says what was on show (the item, its version, the moment, page, heading or rows) and each mark's place in shares of the picture and what it is on: a video's layer and part, a slide's shape, a doc's block, a sheet's range, a 3D object, with the file and line where the editor knows them. Change that element, in its file. An arrow means what its tip points at, and words written on the picture are part of the request. Where a mark names nothing, find it on the picture from its place. The picture is of that moment: if you have changed the piece since, change what it now shows there.
- **Outside Studio,** the person points with your app's own annotation, over `allyhub dev`'s page where your app shows it: Claude's Browser pane has its own (a pen, boxes and text drawn on the page, sent to you with a picture), Codex's browser pane has Annotation Mode. Tell them so when you hand a piece over, in the words your app uses, and never that they cannot mark it up. Only where your app shows the page in no pane of its own (a link opened in the system browser), ask for the moment, the slide, the heading or the cell, and mention that Studio has Mark up, on the desktop or at app.allyhub.com.
- **Render when they ask.** When the person is happy they export it with the editor's Export button, or ask you to. Only then run `allyhub render`, for the files they asked for.
- **To see or operate a web page,** use the browser of the app you run in (Studio's `playwright-cli`, Codex's `@Browser` or `@Chrome`, your host's own tools). AllyHub's headless Chrome is for drawing, and `frames web` only takes a screenshot. With no browser of your own, use Microsoft's `playwright-cli` (`npx @playwright/cli`).
- **Use the cloud tools.** They make the work real: data instead of guesses, a voice or a clip instead of a placeholder. Suggest them when the person has not thought of them.
